Keynote 1 · 2:50–3:25 pm

Binary Program Analysis in the Era of LLM

Chao Zhang

Abstract

Binary analysis is a critical but challenging task in cybersecurity. Comparing to programs with source code, binary programs are intended for machine execution rather than human comprehension. Therefore, the compilers discard unnecessary source code information, e.g., symbol names, types, and even structures like instruction and function boundaries, when generating binaries. The optimization greatly promotes the performance of program size and execution. But it brings many problems for binary analysis tasks. For instance, it is quite challenging to disassemble or decompile binaries, not to mention to recover the semantics of binary programs. In recent years, large language models are widely explored and have shown significant performance in many applications, which brings the question that whether it could promote binary analysis as well. This year, the open source REA agent draws a lot of attentions, which glues many reverse engineering tools and makes binary analysis available for general users. In this talk, the speaker will present a solution Machine Language Model (MLM) and show its application in binary analysis, including call graph recovery, semantic similarity detection, and vulnerability discovery.

Biography

Dr. Chao Zhang is a Tenured Associate Professor and the Vice-Dean of the Institute for Network Sciences and Cyberspace at Tsinghua University. He was a member of the Blue-Lotus CTF team and is now the coach of the team, and has participated in many CTF games. He has also led the CodeJitsu team to participate in DARPA Cyber Grand Challenge, and built an automated system good at binary analysis and defense. His research interests lie in software and system security, especially on automated and intelligent binary analysis. He has published dozens of papers in top-tier security and software conferences, and has received several awards including Tsinghua Young Scholar Award, MIT TR35 China, Qiu Shi Outstanding Young Scholar Award, Japanese Okawa Foundation Research Grant Award, and CCF-IEEE CS Young Computer Scientist Award.

Keynote 2 · 3:25–4:00 pm

Rethinking Fuzzing with LLMs: From Input Generation to Campaign Control

Chenxiong Qian

Abstract

Fuzzing is a powerful technique for discovering software vulnerabilities, but complex input formats and deep program logic continue to limit its effectiveness. Large language models offer new opportunities to overcome these barriers through semantic reasoning and execution feedback. This talk presents two complementary approaches to integrating these capabilities into fuzzing. First, I will introduce R1-Fuzz, which uses reinforcement learning to specialize a compact language model for textual input generation. By learning to reason about program behavior and reach unexplored branches, the model generates inputs that help conventional fuzzers explore deeper program logic. I will then present NAFuzz, which places LLM agents in charge of campaign-level decisions, including seed generation, harness selection and synthesis, and recovery from coverage plateaus. Its multi-agent architecture combines parallel workers and persistent, revisable memory with conventional fuzzing engines that retain responsibility for high-throughput mutation and execution. Drawing on experiments with real-world software, I will discuss how model specialization and agent orchestration address different bottlenecks in vulnerability discovery, and the lessons these systems offer for combining LLM reasoning with efficient, sustained program exploration.

Biography

Chenxiong Qian is an Assistant Professor in the School of Computing and Data Science at The University of Hong Kong, where he leads the System Security Lab. His research focuses on building systems to improve computer security, drawing on programming languages, software engineering, operating systems, and artificial intelligence. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the Georgia Institute of Technology and his bachelor’s degree in Software Engineering from Nanjing University. Before his doctoral studies, he conducted research on Android security at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University under Prof. Daniel Xiapu Luo.

Keynote 3 · 4:00–4:35 pm

Integrating LLM with Program Analysis for Automated Web Application Vulnerability Repair

Wei Meng

Abstract

Effective vulnerability remediation remains challenging despite increasingly sophisticated detection techniques: turning a vulnerability finding into a correct, minimal patch still requires substantial manual effort. LLMs have shown strong promise in automated program repair, but applying them to security vulnerabilities is non-trivial. Existing repair systems often assume specialized toolchains and readily available test suites, and LLMs may lack the precise runtime and data-flow context needed to generate trustworthy fixes.This talk discusses how LLMs and program analysis can be combined to address these gaps. We use vulnerability repair in web applications as an illustrative example, with particular attention to dynamic languages such as PHP. We present SlicePatch, a framework that uses proof-of-concept-driven dynamic profiling to capture runtime behavior and identify vulnerability-specific code paths. Isolated vulnerable code slices distilled from the broader application guide LLMs to generate precise patches while reducing invocation cost. A directed differential fuzzing technique then validates and refines patch candidates iteratively. Our evaluation across 96 real-world vulnerabilities and 7 LLMs shows that SlicePatch attains a 94.79% repair success rate, outperforming baselines by over 30%.

Biography

Wei Meng is an Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the Chinese University of Hong Kong. His main research interests are in computer security and privacy. His research has been published primarily at top conferences such as IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, CCS, ESEC/FSE, ICSE, and WWW. He received his Ph.D. degree in Computer Science from the Georgia Institute of Technology in 2017 and his Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Technology from Tsinghua University in 2012. He currently leads the CUHK Computer Security Lab.

Keynote 4 · 4:35–5:10 pm

Advancing Foundation Models: From Generalization to Controllable Intelligence

Kun Hu

Abstract

Foundation models have demonstrated impressive capabilities across a wide range of domains, providing a powerful starting point for modern AI systems. This talk examines how to further improve their ability to generalize through richer, more transferable representations that capture the underlying structure of complex data. It also explores how these representations can be adapted to the specific demands of real-world tasks, where pretraining alone is often insufficient. As these capabilities grow, so does the need to control unwanted behaviors without compromising useful ones. The final part addresses this challenge through selective concept erasure, investigating how targeted interventions in learned representations can suppress undesirable concepts while preserving useful model capabilities.

Biography

Kun Hu is a Lecturer at School of Science, Edith Cowan University, Australia. He received his Ph.D. in Information Technology from The University of Sydney in 2022. His research focuses on multimedia computing, artificial intelligence, and interdisciplinary studies in science. He has contributed to the field through 80+ top-tier publications, with an h-index of 21. He served as a guest editor for the journals IEEE JSTARS, Remote Sensing. Keynote 5 · 5:10–5:45 pm

LLM-Assisted Vulnerability Discovery: What Role Do Humans Still Play in Testing?

Xiaogang Zhu

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) can generate tests, analyze code, and construct complex inputs, creating new opportunities for automated vulnerability discovery. Yet generating plausible tests is only a starting point: effective testing requires meaningful targets, executable inputs, and reliable evidence of failure. This talk examines how human expertise guides LLM-assisted testing through three questions: How can software fail? How can tests reach the relevant program behavior? And how can LLMs themselves fail? Drawing on recent research, the talk explores how failure scenarios, boundary conditions, program dependencies, and execution feedback help models produce more targeted tests. It also considers code language models as attack targets, using training-data extraction to illustrate the challenges of identifying and validating information leakage. Across these examples, a common theme emerges: automation shifts human effort toward defining objectives, selecting useful context, designing feedback, and interpreting results. The talk offers a perspective on how human insight and LLM capabilities can work together to make vulnerability discovery more effective.

Biography

Xiaogang Zhu is a Lecturer at Adelaide University. He is interested in techniques for bug detection, such as fuzzing, and AI4security. He has published 30+ papers on top conferences and journals such as CCS, NDSS, USENIX, S&P, ASE, ICSE, CVPR, AAAI, TIFS and TDSC. He was a PC at top conferences such as WWW and USENIX Security. He received the Distinguished Paper Award from ASE. He received his PhD degree at Swinburne University of Technology.
